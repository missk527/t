Answer Key – X-rays and Gamma Rays (40 questions)

Answers 1–40

  1. The high-energy end of the EM spectrum.
  2. Very short wavelength.
  3. Very high frequency.
  4. Viewing internal structures (medical imaging).
  5. It passes X-rays through the body and forms an image from what gets through.
  6. A detector plate (or sensor).
  7. Dense materials, especially bone.
  8. Parts that are mostly air (lungs, intestines).
  9. Bones absorb X-rays, so fewer reach the detector and the area stays white.
  10. Air absorbs little, so more X-rays hit the detector and it darkens.
  11. Soft tissues partially absorb X-rays, producing grey shades.
  12. The image starts white and only darkens where radiation reaches the detector.
  13. Broken bones (fractures).
  14. It is ionising radiation that can damage cells/DNA.
  15. The dose is low and the diagnostic benefit is high; it’s quick/cheap.
  16. Staff may be exposed repeatedly over time.
  17. Lead aprons (lead shielding).
  18. They leave the room or stand back during exposure.
  19. Radiotherapy.
  20. Sterilizing equipment and food.
  21. They kill microorganisms.
  22. Medical equipment.
  23. Food.
  24. Boiling water (heat sterilization).
  25. Some materials (e.g., plastics) can be damaged by heat.
  26. They sterilize without high temperatures, reducing heat damage risk.
  27. Fewer/no microbes remain to start spoilage and decomposition.
  28. No—described as killing microbes without altering the food.
  29. Ionising radiation.
  30. It can remove electrons from atoms (ion formation).
  31. Radiation risk vs diagnostic/treatment benefit.
  32. Cancer.
  33. Whether the medical benefit outweighs the risk.
  34. Bone appears white because it absorbs X-rays strongly.
  35. Lung air appears darker because X-rays pass through and darken the detector.
  36. It darkens/blackens where radiation hits it.
  37. Exposure time is short and doses are controlled for patients.
  38. It’s fast and relatively inexpensive.
  39. Risks and benefits vary by patient, condition, and required dose.
  40. X-rays: imaging inside the body; Gamma rays: sterilization (or radiotherapy, but sterilization is emphasized here).
