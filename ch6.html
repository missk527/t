Distillation – Revision Questions

  1. What is distillation used for?
    → To separate mixtures containing liquids.
  2. Name the two main types of distillation.
    → Simple distillation and fractional distillation.
  3. Which method is used to separate a liquid from a dissolved solid?
    → Simple distillation.
  4. Give an example of simple distillation in everyday life.
    → Separating pure water from seawater.
  5. Which method is used to separate mixtures of liquids?
    → Fractional distillation.
  6. Why is simple distillation not suitable for separating two liquids with similar boiling points?
    → Because both liquids would evaporate at similar temperatures.
  7. What property allows liquids to be separated by distillation?
    → Their different boiling points.
  8. Which substance remains in the flask when seawater is distilled?
    → Salt.
  9. What is the main difference between simple and fractional distillation?
    → Fractional distillation uses a fractionating column; simple distillation does not.
  10. Give one large-scale use of fractional distillation.
    → Separating crude oil.

Equipment (Simple Distillation)

  1. What piece of equipment holds the liquid mixture?
    → A flask.
  2. Why must the apparatus be sealed properly?
    → So gas or vapour cannot escape.
  3. Which instrument measures the temperature of the vapour?
    → A thermometer.
  4. What device cools the vapour back into a liquid?
    → A condenser.
  5. What surrounds the condenser to keep it cool?
    → A water jacket.
  6. Why is cold water passed through the jacket from bottom to top?
    → To ensure efficient cooling as water rises and absorbs heat.
  7. Where is the distilled liquid collected?
    → In a beaker at the end of the condenser.
  8. What is used to heat the flask?
    → A Bunsen burner or other heating device.
  9. What happens to the liquid when it is heated strongly?
    → It evaporates.
  10. Why does vapour move into the condenser?
    → Because of pressure and rising vapour.

Simple Distillation Process

  1. What happens to seawater during simple distillation?
    → The water evaporates, leaving salt behind.
  2. Why does vapour condense in the condenser?
    → Because of the cold water in the condenser jacket.
  3. What is the liquid collected called?
    → Distilled (pure) water.
  4. What remains in the flask after distillation?
    → Salt.
  5. Is simple distillation suitable for separating liquids with close boiling points?
    → No, it is not effective.

Fractional Distillation

  1. What extra piece of equipment is used in fractional distillation?
    → A fractionating column.
  2. Why is a fractionating column needed?
    → To create a temperature gradient.
  3. Why is the bottom of the column hotter than the top?
    → Because heat comes from the flask at the bottom.
  4. What feature of the column helps repeated condensation and evaporation?
    → A very high surface area.
  5. Which alcohol has the lowest boiling point in the mixture given?
    → Methanol.
  6. What is the boiling point of methanol?
    → About 65 °C.
  7. Why does ethanol condense higher up the column than propanol?
    → Because its boiling point is lower.
  8. Which alcohol boils at about 78 °C?
    → Ethanol.
  9. Which alcohol has the highest boiling point here?
    → Propanol.
  10. How can propanol be collected after ethanol?
    → Raise the temperature further to boil it off.

True / False

  1. Simple distillation can separate liquids with very similar boiling points.
    → False.
  2. Liquids with higher boiling points condense lower in the fractionating column.
    → True.
  3. Fractional distillation uses repeated evaporation and condensation.
    → True.
  4. Crude oil is separated using fractional distillation.
    → True.
