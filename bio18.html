Bio 18 — Circulatory System

General Overview

  1. The heart
  2. Arteries
  3. Capillaries
  4. Veins
  5. Their structure matches their function (e.g., arteries are strong for high pressure, capillaries are thin for exchange, veins are wide with valves for return flow).

Arteries

  1. Because blood has been pumped out of the ventricles at high pressure.
  2. Thick muscle tissue and elastic tissue.
  3. To withstand high pressure and remain strong.
  4. The hole/space in the middle of a blood vessel.
  5. Artery walls are thicker than the size of their lumen.

Capillaries

  1. To exchange nutrients, oxygen, and waste products with tissues.
  2. To reach all body cells for exchange.
  3. One cell thick.
  4. Substances can easily diffuse through them.
  5. Oxygen, nutrients, carbon dioxide, and waste products.
  6. The lumen of capillaries is very tiny compared to arteries and veins.
  7. Because there are so many capillaries in the body.
  8. Much lower than in arteries.
  9. Blood flows more slowly because of the large cross-sectional area.
  10. It gives more time for exchange of substances with tissues.

Veins

  1. Veins form when capillaries join together.
  2. Large lumens.
  3. Thinner walls than arteries.
  4. Because blood in veins is at low pressure.
  5. Valves.
  6. Toward the heart.
  7. Because the blood has already passed through capillaries and lost pressure.
  8. With the help of valves and sometimes surrounding muscle contractions.
