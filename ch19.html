Chemistry – Carbon Allotropes and Nanotechnology

  1. Allotropes are different structural forms of the same element in the same physical state.
  2. Graphite is a solid form of carbon.
  3. Graphite consists of many stacked layers.
  4. Each single layer of graphite is called graphene.
  5. The carbon atoms in graphene are arranged in hexagons.
  6. Each carbon atom forms three covalent bonds.
  7. Graphene has a strong covalent network arranged in a hexagonal lattice.
  8. Graphene contains delocalised electrons.
  9. These electrons are shared and free to move through the structure.
  10. This makes graphene both very strong and electrically conductive.
  11. Graphene can be obtained from graphite found in the ground.
  12. Carbon nanotubes are a type of fullerene.
  13. Some fullerenes contain pentagons and heptagons.
  14. Fullerenes are hollow carbon structures.
  15. A buckyball is a hollow spherical fullerene made of carbon atoms.
  16. The most common buckyball is called C60.
  17. C60 is made up of 60 carbon atoms.
  18. Fullerenes can surround drug molecules and deliver them to target areas.
  19. This works by enclosing another molecule inside a hollow carbon structure.
  20. Fullerenes have a high surface area to volume ratio.
  21. This means a lot of exposed surface compared to the amount of material.
  22. Carbon nanotubes are tiny tubes made from carbon structures.
  23. They are used at extremely small scales in advanced technology.
  24. Carbon nanotubes can conduct electricity.
  25. Their long, thin shape gives them a high length-to-diameter ratio.
  26. This means they are very long compared to how thin they are.
  27. They can reinforce materials while adding very little mass.
  28. An example use is in tennis racket frames.
  29. Nanotechnology involves structures at the nano-scale.
  30. Nanotechnology is used in medicine.
  31. It is also used in batteries, food, and fashion.
  32. Fullerenes are hollow carbon allotropes with many applications.
  33. Graphene is a single layer of strong, conductive carbon atoms.
  34. Graphene is one layer within graphite’s stacked structure.
  35. Graphene is flat, while fullerenes are curved or hollow.
  36. The strongest carbon allotrope is graphene.
  37. Fullerenes include nanotubes and C60 spheres.
  38. A high surface area provides more sites for chemical reactions.
  39. Graphene’s properties come from strong bonding and delocalised electrons.
  40. This explains the many practical uses of graphene and fullerenes.
