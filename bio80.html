Biology Chapter 80: Mutations and DNA

Introduction to Mutations

  1. A mutation is a change in the DNA base sequence.
  2. The DNA base sequence refers to the order of DNA bases.
  3. Mutations can occur during DNA replication.
  4. DNA replication is the process of copying DNA.
  5. Mutagens are harmful chemicals that increase mutation risk.
  6. Cigarette smoke is an example of a mutagen.
  7. X-rays and gamma rays can also cause mutations.
  8. Not all mutations are harmful.

Genes and Proteins

  1. A gene is a section of DNA coding for a protein.
  2. A codon is a group of three DNA bases.
  3. Each codon contains three bases.
  4. Each codon codes for one amino acid.
  5. Amino acids join together to form a protein chain.
  6. A protein’s function depends on its shape.
  7. Mutations can change amino acid sequences.
  8. If an enzyme changes shape, its active site may also change shape.
  9. The active site is the part of an enzyme where substrates bind.
  10. If the active site changes, the substrate may no longer fit.
  11. Some mutations do not affect proteins.
  12. Proteins may still function normally after some mutations.

Non-Coding DNA and Gene Expression

  1. Some mutations occur in non-coding DNA.
  2. Non-coding DNA is DNA that does not code for proteins.
  3. Mutations in non-coding DNA may not have any effect.
  4. Gene expression means a gene is switched on.
  5. If a gene is not expressed, it is not active.
  6. Some cells do not need hemoglobin.
  7. Red blood cells contain hemoglobin.

Types of Mutations

  1. A substitution mutation occurs when one base is replaced by another.
  2. An insertion mutation occurs when an extra base is added.
  3. Insertions shift later codons in the sequence.
  4. A deletion mutation occurs when a base is removed.
  5. After a deletion, later bases shift position.
  6. A frameshift mutation changes the reading frame of DNA.
  7. Insertions and deletions can both cause frameshift mutations.
  8. Frameshift mutations may produce completely different amino acid sequences.
  9. As a result, proteins may become completely different.
  10. A substitution mutation changes one base in the sequence.
  11. Different amino acids may be coded for after mutation.
  12. Changes in amino acid sequences may change protein function.
  13. Mutations can affect proteins and inherited traits.
