Chemistry – Carboxylic Acids

  1. Carboxylic acids belong to a homologous series.
  2. The functional group of carboxylic acids is the –COOH (carboxyl) group.
  3. Carboxylic acids are named using the suffix “–anoic acid”.
  4. The carboxylic acid with one carbon is methanoic acid.
  5. The carboxylic acid with two carbons is ethanoic acid.
  6. The carboxylic acid with three carbons is propanoic acid.
  7. The carboxylic acid with four carbons is butanoic acid.
  8. In displayed formulas, the carboxyl group is written at the end and kept together as –COOH.
  9. Carboxylic acids are weak acids.
  10. This means only some molecules ionise and release H+ ions.
  11. A reversible reaction is shown using an equilibrium arrow (⇌).
  12. The ion released by an acid is a hydrogen ion (H+).
  13. The ion formed when a carboxylic acid loses H+ is a carboxylate ion (RCOO−).
  14. Carboxylate ions are named using the suffix “–anoate”.
  15. The carboxylate ion from ethanoic acid is the ethanoate ion.
  16. The carboxylate ion from propanoic acid is the propanoate ion.
  17. The general products of carboxylic acid ionisation are RCOO− and H+.
  18. Carboxylic acids react with metal carbonates to form a salt, water, and carbon dioxide.
  19. The gas produced is carbon dioxide (CO2).
  20. The liquid produced is water (H2O).
  21. The salt formed is a metal carboxylate.
  22. An example salt is potassium ethanoate.
  23. Oxidation is used to convert some organic compounds into carboxylic acids.
  24. Alcohols can be oxidised to form carboxylic acids.
  25. Oxidation involves adding oxygen and/or removing hydrogen.
  26. During oxidation, the number of carbon atoms stays the same.
  27. For example, butanol can be oxidised to butanoic acid.
  28. Carboxylic acids are weak because ionisation is reversible and incomplete.
  29. A homologous series is a family of organic compounds with similar structures and predictable trends.
  30. The key functional group is the –COOH group.
  31. The carboxylate ion has an overall negative charge.
  32. The symbol –COOH indicates a carboxylic acid functional group.
  33. The symbol RCOO− indicates the carboxylate ion form.
  34. A metal carbonate reacts with an acid to produce salt + water + carbon dioxide.
  35. This reaction produces a carboxylate salt, water, and carbon dioxide.
  36. Butanol is the alcohol that can be oxidised to butanoic acid.
  37. Ethanoic acid is a common example of a carboxylic acid.
  38. Strong acids fully ionise, while weak acids only partially ionise.
  39. Writing –COOH at the end clearly shows the functional group.
  40. Carboxylic acids can be made by oxidising an alcohol using an oxidising agent.
