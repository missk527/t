Physics Chapter 78: Electromagnetism

Magnetic Fields Around a Wire

  1. An electric current produces a magnetic field.
  2. A current-carrying wire creates a magnetic field around it.
  3. The magnetic field lines around a straight wire form concentric circles.
  4. The magnetic field is strongest close to the wire.
  5. The direction of the magnetic field depends on the current direction.
  6. The right-hand rule is used to find the direction of the magnetic field.
  7. Your thumb shows the current direction.
  8. Your curled fingers show the magnetic field direction.
  9. If the current reverses, the magnetic field reverses.

Magnetic Fields in Coils and Solenoids

  1. A coil is a loop of wire.
  2. The magnetic fields from each loop combine together.
  3. A solenoid is a long coil with many turns.
  4. The magnetic field inside a solenoid is strong and uniform.
  5. A solenoid behaves like a bar magnet.
  6. One end of the solenoid acts as the north pole.
  7. The opposite end acts as the south pole.

Electromagnets

  1. An electromagnet is a magnet made using electricity.
  2. An electromagnet only works while current flows.
  3. If the current stops, the magnetic field disappears.
  4. When current flows again, the magnetic field returns.
  5. Reversing the current reverses the magnetic poles.
  6. The north and south poles swap around.
  7. A single loop of wire produces only a weak magnetic field.

Making Electromagnets Stronger

  1. Increasing the current makes the magnetic field stronger.
  2. Adding more turns to the coil makes the field stronger.
  3. More densely packed coils also increase the field strength.
  4. An iron core can be placed inside the solenoid.
  5. The iron core becomes magnetised.
  6. A material that becomes magnetic in a magnetic field is called a magnetic material.
  7. The iron core becomes an induced magnet.
  8. When the field is removed, soft iron loses its magnetism.
  9. Soft iron is a soft magnetic material.
  10. Electromagnets are useful because they can be switched on and off.
  11. An iron core helps produce a stronger magnetic field.
  12. The magnetic field lines stretch and combine inside a solenoid.

Summary of Electromagnetism

  1. A solenoid is a coil that creates a magnetic field when current flows.
  2. Magnetic field strength can be increased by more current, more turns, denser coils, or adding an iron core.
  3. Reducing the current weakens the magnetic field.
  4. If current direction changes, the magnetic poles swap around.
  5. Electric currents create magnetic fields.
