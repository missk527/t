Chemistry Chapter 74: Potable Water

What Is Potable Water?

  1. Potable water is water that is safe to drink.
  2. Potable water is not pure water.
  3. Pure water contains only H2O molecules.
  4. Potable water should have fairly low levels of dissolved substances.
  5. Potable water usually has a pH between 6.5 and 8.5.
  6. Water is treated to prevent disease and contamination.
  7. Untreated water may contain harmful microorganisms such as bacteria or fungi.

Water Sources

  1. Fresh water has low levels of dissolved substances.
  2. The UK has good fresh water supplies because it receives a lot of rain.
  3. The two main sources of fresh water are surface water and groundwater.
  4. Surface water is found in lakes, rivers, and reservoirs.
  5. Groundwater is water found underground.
  6. An aquifer is permeable rock that stores water.
  7. Surface water is exposed and easy to collect.
  8. Surface water can be reduced because sunlight can evaporate it.
  9. In dry areas, surface water may be limited.

Treating Fresh Water

  1. Fresh water is treated to make it safe to drink.
  2. Wire mesh is used to screen out large objects.
  3. Twigs and other large debris are removed during screening.
  4. Sand and gravel filters remove smaller solid particles.
  5. Sterilisation is used to kill harmful microbes.
  6. Chlorine, ozone, or ultraviolet light can be used to sterilise water.
  7. Chlorine gas is often used for sterilisation.
  8. Ultraviolet light can also kill microorganisms in water.

Desalination

  1. Some countries need desalination because they have little rainfall.
  2. Desalination is the process of removing salt from seawater.
  3. Seawater is useful because there is a huge amount of it.
  4. Desalination requires lots of energy.
  5. Desalination can be carried out by distillation or reverse osmosis.

Distillation

  1. In distillation, seawater is boiled.
  2. Boiling produces water vapour.
  3. The water vapour is then condensed to form fresh water.

Reverse Osmosis

  1. Reverse osmosis is a filtration process using a membrane.
  2. Only water molecules can pass through the membrane.
  3. Ions and larger molecules cannot pass through the membrane.
  4. Reverse osmosis can be used to produce potable water.
  5. Reverse osmosis separates salt from water.
  6. Salt ions are trapped by the membrane.
  7. Distillation is useful but requires heating and energy.
  8. Reverse osmosis is another method used to desalinate seawater.
