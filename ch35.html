Chemistry Chapter 35: Acids, Bases and Salts

Neutralisation Reactions

  1. Neutralisation is a reaction between an acid and a base.
  2. Common bases include metal oxides, metal hydroxides, and metal carbonates.
  3. An acid reacts with a metal oxide to produce salt and water.
  4. An acid reacts with a metal hydroxide to produce salt and water.
  5. An acid reacts with a metal carbonate to produce salt, water, and carbon dioxide.

Naming Salts

  1. The acid provides the negative ion in a salt.
  2. The base provides the positive metal ion in a salt.
  3. Hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide form sodium chloride.
  4. Sulfuric acid and potassium hydroxide form potassium sulfate.
  5. Potassium sulfate has the formula K2SO4 because sulfate is 2− and potassium is 1+.
  6. Nitric acid and calcium hydroxide form calcium nitrate.
  7. Carbon dioxide is produced when acids react with metal carbonates.

Soluble and Insoluble Bases

  1. Soluble means able to dissolve in water.
  2. An insoluble base is a base that does not dissolve in water.
  3. Copper oxide is an example of an insoluble base.

Making a Soluble Salt

  1. The acid is warmed to help the reaction happen.
  2. The insoluble base is added a little at a time.
  3. The solid disappears as it reacts with the acid.
  4. When some solid no longer disappears, the base is in excess.
  5. Excess base is added to make sure all the acid is neutralised.
  6. Filtration is used to remove the excess insoluble solid.
  7. Filter paper and a funnel are used during filtration.
  8. The filtrate contains dissolved soluble salt.
  9. Reacting copper oxide with hydrochloric acid produces copper chloride solution.
  10. To obtain crystals, evaporate some water from the solution and then allow it to cool.
  11. Strong heating should be avoided because it may damage the salt.
  12. A water bath or electric heater can be used to heat the solution safely.
  13. The solution is ready to cool when crystals start forming.
  14. As the solution cools, more crystals form.
  15. The crystals are separated from the remaining solution by filtration.
  16. The crystals can be dried with filter paper or left somewhere warm.
  17. The final product is crystals of a soluble salt.

Common Ions and Formulae

  1. Chloride ions are formed from hydrochloric acid.
  2. Sulfate ions are formed from sulfuric acid.
  3. Nitrate ions are formed from nitric acid.
  4. Some metal ions, such as calcium ions, have a charge of 2+.
  5. Chloride ions have a charge of 1−.
  6. Small numbers in chemical formulae show the correct number of atoms.
  7. A soluble salt is a salt that dissolves in water.
  8. The aim of this practical is to make soluble salt crystals.
