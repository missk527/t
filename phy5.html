Heat Transfer – Revision Questions

  1. What happens to an object’s thermal energy store when it is heated?
    → Energy is transferred to its thermal energy store and its temperature increases.
  2. Name the three methods of heat transfer.
    → Conduction, convection, and radiation.
  3. Why is the medium important in heat transfer?
    → Because each method works differently in solids, fluids, or empty space.
  4. Which method of heat transfer occurs mainly in solids?
    → Conduction.
  5. Which method occurs in liquids and gases?
    → Convection.
  6. Which method can transfer heat through empty space?
    → Radiation.

Conduction

  1. What is the main idea behind conduction?
    → Vibrating particles transfer energy to neighbouring particles.
  2. Where is kinetic energy transferred first when heating a metal rod?
    → To the particles at the heated end.
  3. What causes nearby particles to vibrate faster?
    → Collisions with particles that have gained kinetic energy.
  4. How does energy spread through a solid by conduction?
    → Through repeated collisions passing energy along the solid.
  5. Why does conduction mainly occur in solids?
    → Because particles are close together and collide frequently.
  6. What is thermal conductivity?
    → How well a material transfers energy by conduction.
  7. Why do metals conduct heat quickly?
    → They have a high thermal conductivity.
  8. Why are plastics used as insulators?
    → They have a low thermal conductivity.
  9. How does particle spacing in fluids affect conduction?
    → Particles are further apart, so fewer collisions occur.

Convection

  1. What is meant by a fluid?
    → A substance that can flow, such as a liquid or gas.
  2. Why can convection occur in fluids but not solids?
    → Because particles in fluids can move freely.
  3. What happens to particles when they gain kinetic energy?
    → They move faster and spread out.
  4. How does diffusion help convection?
    → Energetic particles move from warm to cooler regions.
  5. What happens to the density of a fluid when heated?
    → It becomes less dense.
  6. Why do warmer regions of fluid rise?
    → Because they are less dense.
  7. What happens to cooler fluid as warm fluid rises?
    → Cooler fluid sinks to replace it.
  8. What is a convection current?
    → A cycle of warm fluid rising and cool fluid sinking.
  9. Why do convection currents continue while heating occurs?
    → Heating and cooling create a repeating cycle.
  10. Where do convection currents occur in everyday life?
    → In oceans and near radiators in buildings.
  11. How does a radiator produce a convection current?
    → Warm air rises and is replaced by cooler air that is heated in turn.
  12. How does a blanket reduce heat loss by convection?
    → It stops warm air from flowing away.
  13. What is the main difference between conduction and convection?
    → In conduction only energy moves; in convection the particles move.
  14. What do conduction and convection have in common?
    → Both involve particles gaining kinetic energy.

Radiation

  1. How can heat be transferred without particles?
    → By radiation.
  2. What type of waves carry heat in radiation?
    → Infrared waves.
  3. Can radiation travel through a vacuum?
    → Yes.
  4. What do all objects do with radiation?
    → They both emit and absorb radiation.
  5. How does temperature affect radiation emission?
    → Hotter objects emit more radiation.
  6. Why does a barbecue feel hot from a distance?
    → Infrared radiation is emitted and absorbed by your skin.
  7. Which part of the spectrum is infrared in?
    → The infrared region of the electromagnetic spectrum.
  8. What happens during absorption and emission of radiation?
    → Objects both absorb and emit infrared radiation.
  9. How is radiation different from conduction and convection?
    → It does not require particles and can travel through a vacuum.

Application

  1. Why does stopping fluid movement reduce heat loss?
    → Because it prevents convection.
  2. How does heat spread along a metal spoon and why?
    → By conduction, because energy is transferred by particle collisions.
