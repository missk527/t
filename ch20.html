Metallic Bonding and Alloys – Revision Questions

  1. What is metallic bonding?
    → The bonding between metal atoms in a metal lattice.
  2. Between which particles does metallic bonding occur?
    → Between metal atoms.
  3. What happens to outer electrons in metals?
    → They are released and shared throughout the structure.
  4. What charge do metal atoms become when they lose electrons?
    → They lose electrons, leaving a net positive charge.
  5. What are delocalised electrons?
    → Electrons free to move through the metal structure.
  6. What holds metal ions together in a metal?
    → Attraction between positive ions and delocalised electrons.
  7. How strong is metallic bonding?
    → Strong electrostatic attraction.
  8. Why do metals need a lot of energy to melt?
    → Strong bonding needs lots of energy to overcome.
  9. Why do metals usually have high melting points?
    → Strong bonding needs lots of energy to overcome.
  10. What property results from strong metallic bonding?
    → Their melting points are usually high.
  11. Why do metals conduct electricity?
    → Delocalised electrons can move and carry charge.
  12. Which particles carry electrical charge in metals?
    → Delocalised electrons.
  13. Why do metals conduct heat well?
    → Electrons transfer energy efficiently through the lattice.
  14. Which particles transfer heat energy in metals?
    → Delocalised electrons.
  15. What does malleable mean?
    → Able to be bent or hammered into shape.
  16. Why are pure metals malleable?
    → Layers can slide over each other.
  17. What structure do metals have?
    → A regular lattice with mobile electrons.
  18. Why do layers stay together when metals are bent?
    → The electron sea still attracts the ions after sliding.
  19. What is an alloy?
    → A mixture of two or more elements.
  20. How many elements are usually in an alloy?
    → Two.
  21. How are alloys made?
    → By mixing two metals, or a metal with a non-metal.
  22. Why are alloys made?
    → To disrupt the regular structure.
  23. How do different-sized atoms affect the lattice?
    → They distort the lattice and disrupt smooth layers.
  24. Why are alloys harder than pure metals?
    → Layers can’t slide easily, so it resists deformation.
  25. What happens to malleability in alloys?
    → Malleability is reduced.
  26. Which type of bonding forms ionic compounds?
    → Ionic bonding.
  27. Which type of bonding forms molecules?
    → Covalent bonding.
  28. Which type of bonding forms metals?
    → Metallic bonding.
  29. Which metal is the main component of steel?
    → Iron.
  30. What percentage of carbon is in steel?
    → About 1–2%.
  31. Name one metal added to make stainless steel.
    → Chromium (or manganese or vanadium).
  32. Why are alloying elements added to metals?
    → To change or improve properties such as strength or hardness.
  33. Give one use of alloys.
    → Cars.
  34. What is a lattice?
    → Atoms or ions arranged in a repeating pattern.
  35. How does conductivity change in alloys?
    → Conductivity decreases, making them poorer conductors.
  36. Describe the structure of a metal.
    → Positive ions surrounded by mobile delocalised electrons.
  37. What charge do electrons have?
    → Negative.
  38. What charge do metal ions have?
    → Positive.
  39. What property do alloys usually have compared to pure metals?
    → Greater hardness and strength with less bending.
  40. Why are pure metals more malleable than alloys?
    → Their layers slide easily due to a regular structure.
