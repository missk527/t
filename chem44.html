Hydrogen fuel cells

Turning chemical energy into electrical energy through oxidation and reduction.

01 / What is a fuel cell?

A fuel cell is an electrochemical cell that converts chemical energy into electrical energy. A hydrogen fuel cell combines hydrogen with oxygen, producing water and releasing heat.

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

It supplies electricity while fuel and oxygen are supplied.

02 / Cell components

In a fuel cell the anode is negative and the cathode positive. In electrolysis these signs are reversed. Oxidation always occurs at the anode; reduction always occurs at the cathode.

03 / Hydrogen-ion model

This explanation applies to a proton-conducting cell, such as a proton-exchange membrane (PEM) fuel cell.

  1. Hydrogen enters the anode compartment.
  2. Hydrogen is oxidised: it loses electrons, forming hydrogen ions (H⁺).
  3. Electrons travel through the external circuit from anode to cathode, supplying electrical energy.
  4. Hydrogen ions move through the electrolyte to the cathode.
  5. At the cathode, oxygen combines with hydrogen ions and electrons to form water. Heat is also released.

Water leaves through the cathode-side outlet in this model.

Half-equations

A half-equation shows the electrons lost or gained in one electrode reaction.

ReactionHalf-equation
Oxidation at the anode2H₂ → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻
Reduction at the cathodeO₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O

Each oxygen molecule requires four electrons and four hydrogen ions to produce two water molecules.

04 / Alkaline fuel cells

Potassium hydroxide (KOH) solution is an electrolyte used in alkaline fuel cells. Here hydroxide ions (OH⁻), rather than hydrogen ions, carry charge through the electrolyte, moving from cathode to anode.

ReactionHalf-equation
Oxidation at the anode2H₂ + 4OH⁻ → 4H₂O + 4e⁻
Reduction at the cathodeO₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻

Keep the models separate: the hydrogen-ion explanation does not describe a KOH electrolyte. In the alkaline equations, water is formed at the anode and consumed at the cathode; the net product is still water.

05 / Why does electricity flow?

Hydrogen is the fuel being oxidised. Oxidation means loss of electrons; reduction means gain of electrons.

The electrode reactions establish a potential difference (voltage) between the electrodes. When the circuit is complete, this drives electron flow through the external wire and powers the connected device.

Potential difference is a difference in electrical potential, measured in volts. It is more precise than simply calling it a “difference in charge”.

06 / Benefits and limitations

Benefits

  • Hydrogen fuel cells produce water and heat at the point of use, with no direct CO₂ emissions from the hydrogen reaction.
  • They can power vehicles and provide an alternative to combustion engines or battery-only systems.
  • They can keep supplying electricity while fuel is supplied.

Limitations

  • Hydrogen gas takes up considerable storage space unless compressed or liquefied.
  • Hydrogen is flammable; mixtures with air can be explosive.
  • Producing hydrogen requires energy.
  • The environmental impact depends on how hydrogen is produced, including whether fossil fuels are used.

Claims that fuel cells always last longer, are simpler, or have less polluting disposal than batteries depend on the design and materials. Treat these as possible comparisons, rather than universal facts.

07 / Quick recall

Open a question to reveal its answer.

What energy conversion occurs?

Chemical energy → electrical energy, with heat also released.

Which fuel is oxidised?

Hydrogen. It loses electrons.

Where do electrons travel?

Through the external circuit from the anode to the cathode.

What is the overall balanced equation?

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.

What carries charge through a PEM electrolyte?

Hydrogen ions (H⁺).

What carries charge through a KOH electrolyte?

Hydroxide ions (OH⁻), moving from cathode to anode.

Why are the electrode signs different from electrolysis?

A fuel cell generates electrical energy; electrolysis uses an external power supply to drive a reaction. The anode is negative in a fuel cell and positive in electrolysis.

Does using hydrogen guarantee no overall CO₂ emissions?

No. The fuel cell reaction produces no CO₂, but hydrogen production and supply may cause emissions.
