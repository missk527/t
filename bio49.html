Biology Chapter 49: Genetics and Inheritance

Genes and Alleles

  1. Punnett squares show the possible allele combinations in offspring.
  2. An allele is a different form of a gene.
  3. Dominant alleles are written with a capital letter.
  4. Recessive alleles are written with a lowercase letter.
  5. The dominant allele in this example gives normal muscle.
  6. The recessive allele gives muscular mice.
  7. The dominant allele is represented by capital A.
  8. Homozygous means both alleles are the same.
  9. Heterozygous means the alleles are different.
  10. A recessive trait is only expressed when homozygous recessive.

Genotypes and Phenotypes

  1. AA represents a homozygous dominant genotype.
  2. aa represents a homozygous recessive genotype.
  3. Phenotype refers to physical characteristics.
  4. Genotype refers to the allele combination.

Gametes and Meiosis

  1. Gametes are sex cells.
  2. Gametes are formed by meiosis.
  3. Meiosis is the type of cell division that produces gametes.

Punnett Squares

  1. A Punnett square is a grid used to predict offspring genotypes.
  2. One side of the grid shows one parent’s gametes.
  3. The top of the grid shows the other parent’s gametes.
  4. Each box in the grid represents one possible offspring genotype.
  5. A cross between AA and aa produces all Aa offspring.
  6. Heterozygous means having one dominant and one recessive allele.
  7. The phenotype of Aa offspring is normal.
  8. The dominant allele is expressed in heterozygous individuals.

Inheritance Ratios

  1. A heterozygous cross is written as Aa × Aa.
  2. The possible offspring genotypes are AA, Aa, Aa, and aa.
  3. There is one AA genotype.
  4. There are two Aa genotypes.
  5. There is one aa genotype.
  6. The phenotype ratio is 3:1.
  7. The probability of aa offspring is 1 in 4.
  8. This probability can also be written as 25%.
  9. The homozygous recessive genotype is aa.
  10. AA and Aa both show the dominant phenotype.

Environment and Traits

  1. Punnett squares are useful because they are simple and easy to draw.
  2. Punnett squares do not guarantee exact outcomes in real life.
  3. Genes alone do not determine every trait completely.
  4. The environment can change how traits develop.
  5. Examples of environmental factors include food and sleep.
