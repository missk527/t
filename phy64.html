PHY64 – The Electromagnetic Spectrum

  1. The complete range of electromagnetic waves.
  2. They are transverse waves.
  3. The oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer.
  4. About 3 × 108 m/s in a vacuum.
  5. Yes.
  6. The speed can change depending on the medium.
  7. Refraction is a change in direction caused by a change in speed when a wave enters a new medium.
  8. Seven main regions.
  9. They form one continuous spectrum.
  10. Wavelength and frequency.
  11. They are inversely proportional.
  12. As frequency increases, wavelength decreases.
  13. Radio waves.
  14. Gamma rays.
  15. Visible light.
  16. Different wavelengths within the visible range.
  17. Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.
  18. Red has the longest visible wavelength.
  19. Violet has the shortest visible wavelength.
  20. Ultraviolet.
  21. Infrared.
  22. Ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays.
  23. They can be ionising and can damage cells.
  24. Microwaves and radio waves.
  25. Radio → Microwave → Infrared → Visible → Ultraviolet → X-ray → Gamma.
  26. Frequency increases across the spectrum in that order.
  27. Wavelength decreases across the spectrum in that order.
  28. Up to many kilometres.
  29. Less than 10−3 nm (less than a thousandth of a nanometre).
  30. Radioactive decay (nuclear processes).
  31. Electrons dropping to lower energy levels in atoms.
  32. Vibrations of bonds in molecules.
  33. Yes, electromagnetic waves can travel through a vacuum.
  34. They may be reflected, absorbed, or transmitted.
  35. Yes, more than one of these can occur at the same time.
  36. Absorption: the wave’s energy is taken in by the material.
  37. Transmission: the wave passes through the material.
  38. Reflection: the wave bounces off the surface.
  39. Visible light is often used as a reference point because it is familiar.
  40. The regions are labelled ranges with no gaps between them.
