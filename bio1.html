Biology: Cells and Cell Structures

Introduction to Cells

  1. Cells are the smallest unit of life.
  2. Bacteria consist of a single cell, while animals and plants are made of billions or trillions of cells.
  3. Cells are classified into eukaryotic cells and prokaryotic cells.
  4. Animals, plants, fungi, and protists are made of eukaryotic cells.
  5. Bacteria are made of prokaryotic cells.

Eukaryotic vs. Prokaryotic Cells

  1. Eukaryotic cells have a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, making them more complex.
  2. Prokaryotic cells are simpler because they consist of a single cell without a nucleus.
  3. Unicellular means an organism is made of only one cell.
  4. Animals and plants contain eukaryotic cells.
  5. Bacteria contain prokaryotic cells.

Structures Common to Animal and Plant Cells

  1. The cell membrane controls which substances enter and leave the cell.
  2. The nucleus contains DNA and controls cell activities.
  3. Cytoplasm is a jelly-like substance where organelles sit and chemical reactions take place.
  4. Cytoplasm is like water filling a balloon, but with a jelly-like consistency.
  5. Mitochondria provide energy by breaking down sugars during respiration.
  6. Ribosomes make proteins.

Structures Unique to Plant Cells

  1. The rigid cell wall provides support and structure.
  2. The permanent vacuole contains cell sap, a sugary solution of salts and sugars.
  3. The vacuole helps maintain pressure and supports the cell.
  4. Chloroplasts carry out photosynthesis.
  5. Chlorophyll is the green pigment found in chloroplasts.
  6. Plants appear green because chlorophyll absorbs light and reflects green light.

Bacterial Cells

  1. Bacterial cells do not have a nucleus.
  2. Bacterial cells do not contain membrane-bound organelles.
  3. Their DNA is free-floating in the cytoplasm.
  4. Bacteria contain a single circular strand of DNA.
  5. Plasmids are extra small rings of DNA.
  6. Plasmids can help bacteria survive in the presence of antibiotics.
  7. Flagella are threadlike structures that stick out of the cell.
  8. Flagella rotate to propel bacteria forward.

Comparisons

  1. Eukaryotic cells have a nucleus, while prokaryotic cells do not.
  2. The rigid cell wall in plant cells provides strength and prevents bursting.
  3. Animal cells contain a cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria, and ribosomes.
  4. Plant cells also contain a cell wall, permanent vacuole, and chloroplasts.
  5. Bacterial cells contain plasmids, flagella, and a circular DNA strand.
  6. Bacterial DNA is a free-floating circular strand, while eukaryotic DNA is enclosed inside a nucleus.
  7. Animals and plants are multicellular, while bacteria are unicellular.

Broader Thinking and Extension

  1. Cells are specialized for different functions.
  2. Plants need chlorophyll for photosynthesis, while animals do not make food from sunlight.
  3. The simplicity of bacteria allows them to reproduce quickly and adapt easily.
