Biology – Osmosis

  1. This topic explains what osmosis is and how it affects living cells.
  2. Osmosis is a special type of diffusion.
  3. Diffusion is the movement of particles from a region of high concentration to low concentration.
  4. In osmosis, the particles that move are water molecules.
  5. Osmosis occurs across a partially permeable membrane.
  6. Water moves from a region of higher water concentration.
  7. Water moves to a region of lower water concentration.
  8. Water concentration refers to the amount of water compared to dissolved solutes.
  9. Solutes are substances dissolved in water.
  10. Examples of solutes include sugars and salts.
  11. In the experiment, each beaker contains 100 milliliters of solution.
  12. The left beaker contains 1 gram of solute.
  13. The right beaker contains 3 grams of solute.
  14. The right beaker has a lower water concentration.
  15. Water will move into the right beaker.
  16. The beakers are separated by a partially permeable membrane.
  17. Water moves from the left beaker to the right beaker.
  18. Movement stops when both sides have the same water concentration.
  19. In living organisms, osmosis occurs across cell membranes.
  20. The cytoplasm is the fluid inside a cell.
  21. The cytoplasm has many dissolved sugars and salts.
  22. Water usually enters the cell from outside.
  23. If too much water enters an animal cell, it may burst.
  24. Plant cells have a cell wall for support.
  25. A plant cell that has taken in water becomes turgid.
  26. If water leaves a plant cell, it loses pressure.
  27. This happens when the water concentration outside is lower than inside.
  28. A plant cell that has lost water becomes flaccid.
  29. Osmosis depends on differences in water concentration.
  30. Water concentration is related to the amount of solute present.
  31. Osmosis occurs because of water concentration differences.
  32. A partially permeable membrane allows some substances to pass through.
  33. Water molecules are small enough to pass through the membrane.
  34. Solute molecules are often too large to pass through.
  35. Too much water entering a cell can cause it to burst.
  36. Too little water can cause a cell to shrink.
  37. Cells must control water movement to survive.
  38. Osmosis is the diffusion of water molecules.
  39. It requires a partially permeable membrane and a concentration difference.
  40. Osmosis helps regulate water balance in cells.
  41. Osmosis stops when water concentration is equal on both sides.
