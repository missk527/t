Gas Pressure & Particle Model Notes

  1. Gas particles move randomly in all directions.
  2. When particles hit walls, they bounce off.
  3. Collisions produce force.
  4. Pressure is force exerted per unit area.
  5. Formula: pressure = force ÷ area (P = F/A).
  6. Two factors: temperature and concentration (number of particles).
  7. When temperature increases, particles gain energy and move faster.
  8. Kinetic energy increases when heating gas.
  9. Particle speed increases at higher temperature.
  10. Collision frequency increases at higher temperature.
  11. Pressure increases when temperature increases (if volume is constant).
  12. Concentration is the number of particles in a given volume.
  13. When particle number increases, there are more collisions.
  14. Higher concentration → more frequent collisions.
  15. Pressure increases when concentration increases.
  16. When volume decreases, particles have less space.
  17. Smaller volume → particles hit walls more often.
  18. Collisions increase in smaller volume.
  19. Pressure increases when volume decreases.
  20. Temperature stays constant (if only volume changes).
  21. Example: balloon.
  22. A balloon expands when collisions increase.
  23. Pressure doesn’t rise as much because the balloon stretches.
  24. Volume changes instead of pressure in flexible containers.
  25. Yes, pressure can still increase slightly in balloons.
  26. Balloon expansion is limited by material strength (elastic limit).
  27. Force in collisions increases at higher temperature.
  28. Collision number increases at higher temperature.
  29. If collisions increase → pressure increases.
  30. If collision force increases → pressure increases.
  31. Random motion means particles move in unpredictable directions.
  32. No, particles do not move in straight lines forever.
  33. Collisions change particle direction.
  34. During collisions, force is exerted.
  35. If temperature and concentration increase → pressure increases greatly.
  36. If volume increases → pressure decreases.
  37. If particles move slower → pressure decreases.
  38. Pressure and volume are inversely proportional.
  39. The key cause of pressure is collisions of particles with container walls.
  40. Gas behavior is more complex in balloons because the container can expand (variable volume).
