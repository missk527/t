Chemistry Chapter 1: Atomic Structure

Introduction to Atoms

  1. Everything is made of atoms.
  2. The human body contains more than 100 trillion atoms.
  3. An atom consists of a central nucleus surrounded by electrons in shells.
  4. The nucleus contains protons and neutrons.
  5. Electrons move around the nucleus in energy levels or shells.

Subatomic Particles: Mass and Charge

  1. Protons and neutrons both have a relative mass of 1.
  2. Electrons are about 2,000 times smaller in mass than protons and neutrons.
  3. A proton has a charge of +1 (positive).
  4. A neutron has no charge and is neutral.
  5. An electron has a charge of –1 (negative).
  6. You can remember proton is positive because both words start with the letter P.
  7. You can remember neutron is neutral because both words begin similarly.
  8. Protons and electrons are equal in size of charge but opposite in sign.
  9. The electron is the lightest subatomic particle.

Atom Size and Structure

  1. An atom is about 0.1 nanometers in diameter.
  2. Most of an atom is empty space between the nucleus and the outer electrons.
  3. The structure of an atom is often compared to the solar system, with the nucleus like the sun and electrons like planets.
  4. The nucleus is about 10,000 times smaller than the atom itself.
  5. Atoms are too small to be seen clearly at scale.

Neutral Atoms and Ions

  1. An atom is neutral because the number of protons equals the number of electrons.
  2. If an atom gains electrons, it becomes a negative ion.
  3. If an atom loses electrons, it becomes a positive ion.
  4. An atom with an electrical charge is called an ion.
  5. A negative ion may have a charge of –1.
  6. A positive ion may have a charge of +1.
  7. An atom with extra electrons forms a negative ion.
  8. An atom with fewer electrons than protons forms a positive ion.
  9. An ion with two extra electrons has a charge of –2.

Elements and Nuclear Symbols

  1. An element is a substance made from only one type of atom.
  2. The chemical symbol is the one- or two-letter abbreviation for an element, such as O for oxygen.
  3. The atomic number represents the number of protons in an atom.
  4. The mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons.
  5. Oxygen has 8 protons.
  6. Lithium has 3 protons.
  7. The number of neutrons is calculated using: mass number minus atomic number.
  8. Oxygen-16 contains 8 neutrons because 16 – 8 = 8.
  9. Lithium-7 contains 4 neutrons because 7 – 3 = 4.
  10. Mass numbers can vary because elements can exist as different isotopes.
  11. A neutral oxygen atom has 8 electrons.
  12. A neutral lithium atom has 3 electrons.
