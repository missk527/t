Chemistry – Separating Mixtures

Soluble and Insoluble Substances

  1. An insoluble solid is a solid that does not dissolve in a liquid.
  2. It forms a mixture of a liquid and an insoluble solid.
  3. A soluble solid is a solid that dissolves in a liquid.
  4. When a soluble solid dissolves, it forms a solution.
  5. The dissolved solid is called the solute.
  6. The liquid the solute dissolves in is called the solvent.
  7. An example of an insoluble solid in water is sand.
  8. An example of a soluble solid in water is salt (sodium chloride).

Filtration

  1. Filtration is used to separate insoluble solids from liquids.
  2. A sieve or filter paper can be used.
  3. Filter paper has tiny holes that allow liquid through but not solids.
  4. The holes are small enough for liquid particles but too small for solid particles.
  5. A filter funnel holds the filter paper.
  6. The insoluble solid stays behind on the filter paper as residue.
  7. A soluble solid cannot be separated by filtration because it is dissolved.

Evaporation

  1. Evaporation is used to separate a soluble solid from a liquid.
  2. An evaporating dish or crucible is used.
  3. The dish is placed on a tripod.
  4. A Bunsen burner provides heat.
  5. The liquid evaporates.
  6. The solution becomes more concentrated.
  7. Dry crystals of the solid are left behind.
  8. Evaporation is quick and easy.
  9. However, heating can cause decomposition.
  10. The solid may decompose instead of being collected.

Crystallization

  1. Crystallization is used to prevent decomposition of solids.
  2. The solution is placed in an evaporating dish.
  3. It is heated gently.
  4. A water bath can be used for gentle heating.
  5. Heating stops when crystals start to form.
  6. Solids are less soluble at lower temperatures.
  7. The crystals are filtered out.
  8. Filter paper and a funnel are used.
  9. The crystals are dried.
  10. This can be done by leaving them in a warm place or using an oven.

Summary

  1. Filtration separates insoluble solids from liquids.
  2. Evaporation and crystallization separate soluble solids from liquids.
  3. Evaporation removes the solvent.
  4. Crystallization forms solid crystals from solution.
  5. Soluble solids pass through filter paper because they are dissolved.
