Bio6 - Diffusion

  1. Diffusion is the overall movement of particles from a region of high concentration to a region of low concentration.
  2. Particles move from high concentration to low concentration.
  3. It refers to the net movement of many particles, not individual random motion.
  4. No energy is required.
  5. Diffusion occurs in gases and liquids.
  6. An example is perfume spreading through a room.
  7. Another example is food colouring spreading in water.
  8. Particles eventually spread out evenly.
  9. This happens because particles move randomly due to kinetic energy.
  10. Diffusion is a passive process.

Diffusion and Cell Membranes

  1. Cell membranes are partially permeable (selectively permeable).
  2. Small dissolved molecules can pass through.
  3. Examples include water, glucose, and amino acids.
  4. Large molecules such as proteins and starch cannot diffuse easily.
  5. This is because they are too large to pass through the membrane.
  6. The membrane is selective, allowing only certain substances through.
  7. This allows nutrients like glucose and oxygen to enter cells.
  8. It also allows waste products such as carbon dioxide to leave cells.
  9. The membrane controls what diffuses in and out of the cell.
  10. This ensures cells get what they need and remove waste.

Factors Affecting the Rate of Diffusion

  1. The concentration gradient.
  2. A larger concentration difference increases diffusion rate.
  3. A smaller concentration difference decreases diffusion rate.
  4. Higher temperature increases kinetic energy of particles.
  5. This increases the rate of diffusion.
  6. Lower temperature decreases the rate of diffusion.
  7. A larger surface area increases diffusion rate.
  8. This is because more particles can cross at the same time.
  9. The object with the larger surface area diffuses faster.
  10. Concentration gradient, temperature, and surface area affect diffusion.

True or False

  1. False – diffusion does not require energy.
  2. False – diffusion happens in both gases and liquids.
  3. False – net movement refers to overall movement.
  4. False – large molecules do not diffuse easily.
  5. True – higher temperature increases diffusion rate.

Application and Critical Thinking

  1. Gas particles spread randomly from high to low concentration until evenly distributed.
  2. Dye molecules move randomly and spread throughout the water.
  3. Small cells have a larger surface area to volume ratio, allowing faster diffusion.
  4. In the lungs, oxygen diffuses into the blood while carbon dioxide diffuses out.
  5. In the small intestine, nutrients like glucose diffuse from the gut into the blood.
