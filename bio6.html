Biology – Diffusion

What is Diffusion?

  1. Diffusion is the net movement of particles from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration.
  2. Particles move from high concentration to low concentration.
  3. Net movement means the overall movement of particles, not individual random motion.
  4. Diffusion does not require energy.
  5. Diffusion occurs in gases and liquids.
  6. An example is perfume spreading through a room.
  7. Another example is food colouring spreading in water.
  8. Eventually, particles become evenly distributed.
  9. This happens because particles move randomly due to kinetic energy.
  10. Diffusion is a passive process.

Diffusion and Cell Membranes

  1. Cell membranes are partially (selectively) permeable.
  2. Small, dissolved molecules can pass through.
  3. Examples include water, glucose, and amino acids.
  4. Large molecules such as proteins and starch cannot pass through easily.
  5. This is because they are too large to fit through the membrane.
  6. The membrane is selective and only allows certain substances through.
  7. Diffusion allows nutrients like glucose and oxygen to enter cells.
  8. It allows waste products like carbon dioxide to leave cells.
  9. The membrane controls what diffuses in and out.
  10. This ensures cells get essential substances and remove waste.

Factors Affecting the Rate of Diffusion

  1. The concentration gradient affects diffusion rate.
  2. A larger concentration difference increases diffusion rate.
  3. A smaller concentration difference decreases diffusion rate.
  4. Higher temperature gives particles more kinetic energy.
  5. This increases the rate of diffusion.
  6. Lower temperature decreases the rate of diffusion.
  7. A larger surface area increases diffusion rate.
  8. This is because more particles can diffuse at the same time.
  9. The object with the larger surface area diffuses faster.
  10. Key factors are concentration gradient, temperature, and surface area.

True / False

  1. False – diffusion does not require energy.
  2. False – diffusion occurs in both gases and liquids.
  3. False – net movement refers to overall movement.
  4. False – large molecules do not diffuse easily.
  5. True – higher temperature increases diffusion rate.

Applications of Diffusion

  1. Smells spread because gas particles diffuse from high to low concentration.
  2. Dye spreads in water due to random motion of particles.
  3. Small cells diffuse faster due to a high surface area to volume ratio.
  4. In the lungs, oxygen diffuses into the blood and carbon dioxide diffuses out.
  5. In the small intestine, nutrients such as glucose diffuse into the blood.
